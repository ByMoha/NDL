# English Style, Point of View & Etiquette

Nusuk writes **Plain English** — simple sentences, one idea each, active voice, universal
vocabulary. A Muslim in Lagos, London, Kuala Lumpur, or Chicago should understand it
immediately. Plain is not cold: warmth and plainness are not in tension.

- ✗ "Kindly be informed that transportation arrangements shall commence shortly."
- ✗ "Heads up! Your ride's about to roll 🚌"
- ✓ "Your transport departs in 20 minutes."

Avoid jargon, idioms that don't travel ("you're all set", "piece of cake"), and corporate
abstraction (`leverage`, `utilize`, `facilitate`, `going forward`).

---

## Capitalisation

**Sentence case everywhere** — page titles, headings, buttons, labels, navigation.

> "Confirm booking" — not "Confirm Booking".
> "Your booking details" — not "Your Booking Details".

- **Capitalise proper nouns**: Nusuk, Hajj, Umrah, Tawaf, Salah, Makkah, Madinah, The Prophet ﷺ, Allah.
- **Capitalise branded names**: Nusuk Marketplace, Nusuk ID, Nafath.
- **Lowercase generic features**: hotel booking, visa tracking, prayer times.

The exception: an OS pattern that mandates title case on a platform control. Follow the
platform there, and stay sentence case everywhere else.

---

## Word choices that are settled

- **"Women" and "Men"** — never "Females" and "Males".
- **"Orders"** for confirmed bookings — never "Requests". A request is something still being
  considered; an order is something the user has secured, and the difference matters to someone
  checking whether their Hajj place is real.

---

## Point of view

- **Body text and guidance: second person.** "Your booking is confirmed." "You can pray
  anywhere in the Haram."
- **Buttons and actions: verb first** — "Confirm booking", "Try again", "Browse packages".
  Switch to **first person when the user is making a commitment**: "I agree to the terms."
- **"We" for Nusuk** when describing something Nusuk is doing: "We will notify you when it is
  complete." "We've issued your permit."
- **Never "user" in direct address.** For the pilgrim, Umrah performer, or visitor, the
  approved term is **Guest of the Most Merciful** (ضيف الرحمن). See `glossary.md`.

---

## Information priority

- **Lead with the user's goal, not the system action.** "Your booking is confirmed" — not
  "The system has processed your booking."
- Most important message first, in placement, size, and style.
- Start with what matters to the user, not with what the UI requires of them.

---

## Tense

- **Present tense by default**: "Your visa is ready" — not "Your visa will be ready."
- **Simple past for completed events**: "You completed Tawaf" — not "You've completed Tawaf."
- **Future only when necessary for clarity**: "We will notify you when it is complete."

---

## Please, thank you, and sorry

**"Please"** — use when asking the user to do something; skip when stating facts.

- ✓ "Please check your connection."
- ✗ "Please note that your booking is confirmed." → just "Your booking is confirmed."

**"Thank you"** — after the user does something for us: cooperation, patience. Don't overuse;
it loses meaning.

**"Sorry" / "We regret"** — for genuine inconvenience: a cancelled visit, a system failure.
Don't apologise for routine actions.

- ✗ "Sorry, we need your ID."

---

## Contractions

- **OK in logistical copy** — "You're almost there." "We'll notify you." "Don't forget to check
  your booking."
- **Avoid in faith-led or sacred moments** — "You have completed your Umrah", not "You've
  completed your Umrah."
- **Never in legal or consent text.**

---

## Punctuation

- **Oxford comma: yes.** "Hajj, Umrah, and Tawaf."
- **Exclamation marks: sparingly.** Never in errors. Never more than one. Never beside Quranic
  text or hadith.
- **Ampersands: no** in body text; acceptable in design assets.
- Headings take no end punctuation unless they're a question.
- Quote a button name in single quotes when referring to it: "By clicking 'Pay', you confirm…"
- Ellipsis for in-progress states: "Preparing your booking…"

---

## Numbers and dates

| Element | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Numerals in text | Use figures | `7 nights`, `3 packages` |
| Start of sentence | Spell out | `Seven nights are included.` |
| Over 999 | Comma separator | `1,000` · `150,000` |
| Currency | Symbol or code before the amount | `SAR 500` · `$20` |
| Time | 12-hour with AM/PM | `2:30 PM` |
| On the hour | No minutes | `7 PM` |
| Dates | Weekday, day month | `Saturday, 24 January` |
| Dates, tight space | Abbreviate | `Sat, 24 Jan` |
| Both calendars | **Hijri first** | `10 Dhul Hijjah 1446 / 7 June 2025` |

## Islamic terminology

Retain Arabic terms where no true English equivalent exists. Capitalise them, use the most
widely recognised transliteration, prioritise meaning over literalism, and stay consistent —
inconsistency undermines trust more than an imperfect choice does.

The canonical list is `glossary.md`. The decisions that come up most:

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| Makkah, Madinah | Mecca, Medina |
| Quran | Koran, Qur'an, Holy Quran |
| Circuit (of Tawaf or Sa'i) | Round, Lap |
| Rukn, Wajib, Mustahab, Fidyah | (translate only in plain paraphrase) |
| The Prophet ﷺ | The Prophet (no honorific), (ص) |
| Book your Umrah journey / your Rawdah visit | Permit issuance, apply for a permit |

Don't italicise these — they're the vocabulary of the domain, not foreign words to this audience.

**Honorifics:** `ﷺ` after mentions of the Prophet, always in full. `(SWT)` after Allah in
faith-led copy; in short microcopy `Allah` alone is acceptable.

## Emojis (both languages)

Not banned, not default.

**OK:**
- Logistical notifications where they add clarity — ✅ booking confirmed, 📦 package ready.
- Celebratory logistical moments — a completed booking, a welcome-back screen.
- **Always as enhancement, never as replacement.** The message must make sense without it.

**Never:**
- **Sacred or faith-led moments.** No emoji on Umrah completion, Tawaf guidance, Rawdah booking,
  or any worship content.
- **Error messages.** No 😢 or ⚠️ — they trivialise the moment.
- **Consolation moments.** A cancelled Rawdah visit is not a 💔 moment in the UI.
- **Legal, consent, or identity verification screens.**

**How:** one per message maximum · at the start or end, never mid-sentence · universally
recognised glyphs that render consistently · check contrast in light and dark mode.

## Words to avoid, and what to write instead

| Avoid | Write |
|---|---|
| Kindly | Please |
| Utilize / leverage | Use |
| Commence / initiate | Start |
| Prior to | Before |
| In order to | To |
| Please be advised / be informed | (delete — say the thing) |
| At your earliest convenience | By [date] |
| We apologize for any inconvenience caused | One plain apology, or none |
| Invalid | Doesn't match, isn't recognised |
| Successfully completed | Completed |
| Click here | The action — "Read the booking guide" |
| Submit / OK | The action — "Confirm booking" |
| Permit issuance / apply for | Book your journey, book your visit |
| Login (as a heading) | Sign in |
| Register | Create account |
| Rituals | Manasik |
| Don't miss out / Last chance / Hurry | (forbidden — manufactured urgency) |
| The best ever / world-class | (forbidden — exaggeration) |
| Simply / just / easily | (delete — if it were easy the user wouldn't be stuck) |

`Please` is kept where `Kindly` is not: it's the English counterpart of يرجى and carries the
courtesy baseline. What's forbidden is the bureaucratic stack — "Kindly be advised that you are
requested to…" — not the word.
