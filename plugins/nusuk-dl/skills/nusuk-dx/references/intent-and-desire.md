# Intent vs Desire

> In some cases it is intent. In many others, it is desire.

Nusuk holds a special place in each Muslim's life. The app is designed to provide the most
spiritual experience possible, balancing business success factors with religious feelings and
emotions. **That dual focus is the unique challenge**, and it is why every design decision starts
here.

Getting this classification wrong produces a feature that works and still fails — a habit surface
built like a booking funnel, or a booking flow built like a habit loop.

---

## The distinction

**Desire** — a strong feeling of wanting something.
- **Emotional in nature** — it stems from wishes, longings, or cravings
- **Can exist without action** — desire doesn't require a plan
- Often abstract or idealistic
- *"I desire to be a world-class musician."*

> It reflects what you want — not necessarily what you'll do.

**Intent** — a deliberate decision or mental commitment to act in a certain way.
- **Cognitive and purposeful** — it stems from planning and decision-making
- **Implies readiness to act** — often tied to actual behaviour
- Specific and actionable
- *"I intend to practice piano every day at 6 AM to join the conservatory."*

> It reflects what you plan to do — regardless of emotional desire.

| Aspect | Desire | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Core nature | Emotional | Rational / deliberate |
| Requires action? | No | Often, yes |
| Clarity | Can be vague or abstract | Usually clear and defined |
| Commitment level | Low — a feeling | High — a decision |
| Triggered by | Wants, dreams, fantasies | Goals, planning, values |
| Leads to behaviour? | Not necessarily | Often yes, or at least preparation |

## The typology at Nusuk

Five layers, moving from deep intrinsic desires to functional, action-driven intents.

### 1 — Spiritual desires (core)
Deep, often lifelong inner longings that motivate the journey.

- Purification and forgiveness — seeking mercy, wiping away sins
- Connection with the Divine — feeling closeness to God, being "chosen"
- Fulfilling a pillar of Islam — a sacred obligation for many
- Experiencing spiritual rebirth — inner renewal or a new life chapter
- Longing for sacred history — walking where prophets walked

> These desires are mostly existential, emotional, and transformative.
> **Design implication: interfaces that feel reverent and contemplative, not purely functional.**

### 2 — Emotional-relational desires
Connected to family, belonging, and emotional states.

- Going with loved ones — parents, spouse, or community
- Fulfilling a parent's dream — going on their behalf or with them
- Legacy and remembrance — "leaving behind a good memory"
- Peace and emotional healing — escaping life's chaos or grief

> These desires shape **how people plan their journey and who they include.** A flow that assumes
> a solo traveller is designed against this layer.

### 3 — Transformational intents
Life-phase goals tied to identity, transformation, or healing.

- Turning a new leaf — after addiction, trauma, or major life crisis
- Marking a milestone — retirement, after childbirth, marriage
- Spiritual accountability — making peace before death, illness
- Reclaiming faith — after doubt, distance, or moral struggle

> These are intent-driven, where **the act of going is itself a decision to change.**

### 4 — Journey & logistics intents
Clear actions or plans needed to fulfil the experience.

- Booking Hajj/Umrah slots — managing quotas, schedules
- Securing permits and approvals — for international or local pilgrims
- Navigating the rituals — what to do, when, how
- Ensuring accessibility — elderly, disabled, or medically complex pilgrims
- Translating rituals into a native language — especially for new Muslims

> Transactional, but **crucial for flow and confidence.**

### 5 — Optimisation & support intents
Efforts to enhance, share, or support the experience.

- Tracking progress of rituals — Tawaf, Sa'i
- Getting reminders or spiritual nudges — "It's time for Maghrib"
- Sharing spiritual moments — with family or social media, if appropriate
- Seeking fatwas or guidance — in case of confusion
- Providing help to others — volunteers, interpreters, guides

> These intents are about **agency, clarity, and contribution.**

---

## The five motivational realms

| Realm | Type of motivation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Spiritual** | Intrinsic, faith-based | Forgiveness, Divine closeness |
| Emotional | Relational, identity-driven | Going with parents, peace, legacy |
| **Transformational** | Life-shift, growth | Rebirth, repentance, marking milestones |
| Functional | Task-oriented | Booking, rituals, navigation |
| **Supportive** | Enhancing experience | Reminders, guides, sharing |

## Using this in design

**Ask first: which layer is this feature actually serving?** Not which layer the brief mentions —
which one the user is in when they open the screen.

A booking flow sits in layer 4, but the person using it is frequently in layer 1 or 3. That gap is
the design problem: **the interface must be functionally efficient and emotionally appropriate at
the same time.** Efficiency alone reads as cold; reverence alone reads as obstruction.

**Practical consequences:**

- **Layers 1–2 (desire)** — habit and identity architecture. Patterns: present bias, self-control
  and commitment, identity and autonomy. Metrics: loop repeat rate, routine stability,
  return-after-lapse. The tone is reverent, the pace is the user's.
- **Layers 3 (transformational)** — sits between. High intrinsic motivation, low familiarity.
  Support the decision without amplifying pressure on it.
- **Layers 4–5 (intent)** — journey architecture. Patterns: cognitive load, feedback and
  reinforcement. Metrics: time-to-first-meaningful-action, completion, drop-off points. The tone
  is clear and confident, the pace is fast.

**The returning-pilgrim moment is the highest-value handoff in the product**: a pilgrim who has
just completed a rite has peak intrinsic motivation and no journey left. Converting a journey
habit into a daily habit belongs there, and it is a layer-4 user becoming a layer-1 user in a
single moment.
