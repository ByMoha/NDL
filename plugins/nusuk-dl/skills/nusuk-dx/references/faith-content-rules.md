# Nusuk Faith Content Rules

How to **phrase** UX microcopy when the content team classifies a piece of guidance as rukn, wajib, mustahab, or ikhtilaf. The microcopy writer does not research fiqh, determine ruling categories, or decide how to handle scholarly differences. That is the content team's job.

**The workflow is always:**
1. The content team provides the content and its classification (rukn / wajib / mustahab / ikhtilaf / mustahab).
2. The content team specifies whether ikhtilaf applies and which position to lead with.
3. The microcopy writer phrases it using the patterns below.
4. If a string has no classification from the content team, ask for one. Never guess.

---

## Phrasing by Ruling Type

### When the content team says: Rukn (Pillar)

The act invalidates worship if missed. **Phrase it as a calm, factual statement.** No alarm, no exclamation marks, no commanding tone. The word "rukn" carries its own weight.

| | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN | "Tawaf is a rukn of Umrah." | "You must do Tawaf or your Umrah is invalid!" |
| AR | "الطواف ركن من أركان العمرة." | "يجب عليك الطواف وإلا بطلت عمرتك!" |

**Pattern:** `[Act] is a rukn of [worship].` / `[الفعل] ركن من أركان [العبادة].`

### When the content team says: Wajib (Required)

Missing it requires fidyah, not invalidation. **Phrase it with clarity** — the user should understand it's expected without confusing it with a rukn.

| | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN | "Shaving or trimming the hair is wajib in Umrah." | "You must shave your hair." |
| EN (with consequence, if content team provides it) | "Shaving or trimming the hair is wajib in Umrah. If missed, a Fidyah is required." | "Warning: failure to shave will require compensation." |
| AR | "الحلق أو التقصير واجب في العمرة." | "يجب عليك حلق شعرك." |
| AR (with consequence) | "الحلق أو التقصير واجب في العمرة. في حال تركه، تجب الفدية." | "تحذير: عدم الحلق يستوجب فدية." |

**Pattern:** `[Act] is wajib in [worship].` / `[الفعل] واجب في [العبادة].`
Add the consequence only if the content team provides it, stated factually.

### When the content team says: Mustahab (Recommended)

Extra reward, not required. **Phrase it as a warm invitation — a gift being offered, not a task being added.** This is where Nusuk's hospitable voice leads.

| | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN | "For extra reward, you can make Duaa at the Multazam." | "You should also make Duaa at the Multazam." |
| EN | "Many pilgrims recite Duaa when they first see the Kaaba." | "It is recommended to recite Duaa when you see the Kaaba." |
| AR | "لمزيد من الأجر، يمكنك الدعاء عند الملتزم." | "يجب عليك أيضًا الدعاء عند الملتزم." |
| AR | "يحرص كثير من الحجاج على الدعاء عند رؤية الكعبة لأول مرة." | "يُستحب الدعاء عند رؤية الكعبة." |

**Invitation phrases (EN):** "You can also…" · "For extra reward, consider…" · "Many pilgrims…"
**Invitation phrases (AR):** "يمكنك أيضًا…" · "لمزيد من الأجر، يمكنك…" · "يحرص كثير من الحجاج على…"

Never use "you should," "you must," or "يجب" for mustahab acts.

### When the content team says: Ikhtilaf applies

The content team will tell you which position to lead with (typically the one that is easier, safer, or more practical for the user). **Phrase the chosen position as a positive, practical statement.** Do not explain the ikhtilaf. Do not mention the other opinion. Do not say "must."

| | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN | "You can pray your Nafl Salah anywhere in the Haram after Tawaf." | "You must pray behind Maqam Ibrahim." |
| EN | "You can combine Dhuhr and Asr prayers at Arafat." | "Scholars differ on whether you should combine prayers." |
| AR | "يمكنك صلاة النافلة في أي مكان من الحرم بعد الطواف." | "يجب الصلاة خلف مقام إبراهيم." |
| AR | "يمكنك الجمع بين صلاتي الظهر والعصر في عرفة." | "اختلف العلماء في جواز الجمع…" |

**Pattern:** State the easier/chosen option positively with "You can…" / "يمكنك…" — never frame it as a must, never explain the disagreement, never declare any opinion wrong.

## Quranic and Hadith References in Microcopy

The content team may provide a Quranic ayah or hadith to accompany a string. When they do:

- **Use the exact transmitted wording.** Never paraphrase, summarise, or substitute words in Quranic text or hadith. (E.g., "روضة من رياض الجنة" — never "حديقة من حدائق الجنة.")
- **Place it as context**, not as a proof or a command. It enriches the moment.
- **No emojis** next to Quranic text or hadith.
- **Worship contexts only.** Quranic/hadith references belong near worship guidance (before Tawaf, at Arafat, entering the Rawdah). Do not attach them to transactional screens (payment, verification, form fields).

If the content team has not provided a specific ayah or hadith, do not source one yourself.

## Attribution

- The Council of Senior Scholars is **not cited on every string**. The app speaks with its own voice.
- The source lives once in a visible, persistent place (e.g., About screen, footer on worship-guidance screens).
- The microcopy writer does not need to add attribution to individual strings unless the content team explicitly requests it.

## Summary for Writers

| Content team says | Your phrasing job |
|---|---|
| Rukn | State as calm fact. "[Act] is a rukn." No alarm. |
| Wajib | State clearly. "[Act] is wajib." Add consequence only if provided. |
| Mustahab | Warm invitation. "You can…" / "For extra reward…" Never "you should." |
| Ikhtilaf — lead with X | State X positively. "You can [X]." Don't explain the disagreement. |
| Include this ayah/hadith | Use exact wording. Place as context, not proof. Worship screens only. |
| No classification provided | Ask the content team. Never guess a ruling. |

## Citation Formatting

When the content team supplies an ayah or hadith, format it this way. (Sourcing remains their
job — see above. This section covers presentation only.)

1. **Ayat** sit between ﴿ ﴾ with the sura name and verse number:
   ﴿إِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا﴾ (الشرح: 6)
2. **Hadith** sit between « » with the short source:
   «من حج فلم يرفث ولم يفسق رجع كيوم ولدته أمه» (رواه البخاري ومسلم)
3. **Never alter or abbreviate** in a way that changes meaning. Partial omission with (…) is
   acceptable only where meaning survives intact.
4. **ﷺ** in full, always — never the abbreviation (ص).
5. **Keep scripture separate from promotional text.** Never fuse an ayah into a marketing
   sentence, and never use one as a headline for an offer or a discount.
6. **One ayah or one hadith** per notification or short piece.
7. Diacritics (تشكيل) are kept on Quranic text, unlike the rest of Nusuk's Arabic.
8. **Separate sacred text from marketing copy** — never blend them in a way that weakens the
   dignity of the text.

If you cannot verify a reference exactly, leave the slot empty and flag it. A wrong attribution
is worse than an absent one.

## Long-Form Religious Content

The same rules govern articles and educational content in Zad and Explore, with two additions:

- Every piece is marked **requiring human religious review before publication**, regardless of
  how confident the draft is.
- Where the platform requires it, attribute the article to its reviewed source. Individual
  microcopy strings still don't carry attribution — see above.

---

## Madhab Neutrality

Nusuk remains **madhab-neutral** when guiding on faith. We never pick a side.

All faith-led guidance is simplified, made madhab-neutral, and drawn from one source: **The
Council of Senior Scholars of Saudi Arabia**. Where a difference of opinion exists, the content
team names the position to lead with, and the writer states it positively without explaining the
disagreement (see **Ikhtilaf** above).

Attribution to the Council belongs on About and policy pages — the full name, once, in a visible
persistent place. It does not go on every string.

## Verification Sources

All sharia content is reviewed **both linguistically and religiously** before publication, and
relies on trusted sources — **Dorar Saniyya** (الدرر السنية) among them.

The verification rule above applies here without exception.

## Blessing Vocabulary

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| **AlhamduLillah** / الحمد لله | Affirmative moments — Umrah complete, booking confirmed, rite complete |
| **InshaAllah** / إن شاء الله · بإذن الله | Confirming an expectation on a faith-related item: "We will notify you when it is complete, inshaAllah." |
| **Taqabbal Allah** / تقبل الله طاعتك | On completing a rite: "Mabrouk, your booking is confirmed! May Allah accept your worship." |
| **May Allah grant you acceptance** / نسأل الله لك القبول | Praying for the guest: "Your Umrah journey is near, may Allah grant you acceptance." |

**The rule:** natural placement, no direct preaching, no exaggeration, and **never in purely
logistical messages.** A blessing word on a payment receipt reads as deflection, not warmth.

Don't use `inshaAllah` about something Nusuk itself controls and already knows. Either give the
time, or say plainly what's known.

## In Notifications

Scripture in notifications carries three extra rules — context-bound, one text only, and
placement that follows whether the text is the core of the message or supports it. See
`notifications.md`.
