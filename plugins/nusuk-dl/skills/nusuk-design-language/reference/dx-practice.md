# DX practice

A squad of dreamers and doers, crafting joy-filled experiences across phygital realms. We target **200M engaged users, not installs**, and build for Muslims across the globe with two objectives: **fulfilling intention** and **enlightening desire**. This practice is mandatory for every DX member; the full guide lives in Notion (*Nusuk — The Guide*).

## 1 · Intent vs desire: classify before you draw

Nusuk balances business success with religious feeling. Classify every feature first: is it serving an intent, a desire, or both?

| Aspect | Desire | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Core nature | Emotional | Rational, deliberate |
| Requires action? | No | Often, yes |
| Clarity | Can be vague or abstract | Clear and defined |
| Commitment | Low: a feeling | High: a decision |
| Triggered by | Wants, dreams | Goals, planning, values |
| Leads to behaviour? | Not necessarily | Often, or at least preparation |

**Five layers, from deep to functional**

1. **Spiritual desires (core):** purification and forgiveness, closeness to Allah, fulfilling a pillar of Islam, spiritual rebirth, walking where the prophets walked. *Design: reverent and contemplative, not purely functional.*
2. **Emotional-relational desires:** going with loved ones, fulfilling a parent's dream, legacy and remembrance, peace and healing. *Shapes how people plan and who they include.*
3. **Transformational intents:** turning a new leaf, marking a milestone, spiritual accountability, reclaiming faith. *Going is itself a decision to change.*
4. **Journey and logistics intents:** booking slots, securing permits, navigating rituals, accessibility, translation for new Muslims. *Transactional, but crucial for flow and confidence.*
5. **Optimisation and support intents:** tracking Tawaf and Sa'i, reminders ("It's time for Maghrib"), sharing moments, seeking guidance, helping others. *Agency, clarity, contribution.*

| Motivational realm | Type of motivation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Spiritual | Intrinsic, faith-based | Forgiveness, closeness to Allah |
| Emotional | Relational, identity | Going with parents, peace, legacy |
| Transformational | Life shift | Rebirth, repentance, milestones |
| Functional | Task-oriented | Booking, rituals, navigation |
| Supportive | Enhancing | Reminders, guides, sharing |

## 2 · Principles: Intuitive, Engaging, Intelligent

Dieter Rams is at the core. By hook or by book, reach the objective and keep to the principles.

- **Intuitive.** People feel confident in every decision and never feel lost. Enough information to guide the next step, in a clean, minimal interface built on universal best practice.
- **Engaging.** Every piece engages: actionable engagement (the user acts) or informative engagement (the user learns). Passive screens are a failure.
- **Intelligent.** Data, AI and what we know about the user adapt the interface, improve navigation and prevent overload. The platform acts before the user has to.

Behavioural economics is what we observe. Psychology is why it happens.

## 3 · Intuitive design in eight steps

Based on Everett N. McKay, *Intuitive Design: Eight Steps to an Intuitive UI*.

1. **Understand the context:** surveys, interviews and observation; personas and scenarios.
2. **Simplify the interaction:** task analysis and minimal design; remove clutter to cut cognitive load.
3. **Ensure consistency:** design guidelines and pattern libraries. This is what DL is for.
4. **Clear affordances:** visual cues for what is interactive, plus feedback such as highlight and motion.
5. **Familiar patterns:** use established conventions. Novelty that confuses is a cost.
6. **Immediate feedback:** respond to every press and submit; show progress for anything that takes time.
7. **Design for recovery:** undo and redo, and errors that say what happened and how to fix it.
8. **Test with real users:** iterate with usability testing until the flow needs no explanation.

**The affordance rule:** if an element looks interactive, it must be interactive. A box around text that can't be tapped is a broken promise.

## 4 · The 3Ms: Monitor, Map, Measure

Simplification is the ultimate sophistication: a process that is maintainable, doable, understandable and efficient.

- **M1 Monitor.** Two entry points: Demand (business requirements) or Research (internal insight or initiative). *Mobilize* (receive the request or uncover the insight), *Mull* (analyse until you understand it), *Mold* (craft the case that leads the next phase). **Output:** the Project Canvas — the ask, the impact, the objectives.
- **M2 Map.** Map the experience and build a solid conceptual model that fulfils the experience strategy and metrics. Apply intent vs desire and the behavioural patterns here. **Rule:** no wireframe or prototype starts without a mapped conceptual model.
- **M3 Measure.** Define success criteria up front from the KPI framework, then build the interactive prototype and hand it to development. **Output:** locked metrics, prototype, handoff.

Under time pressure, go through the minimum list of practices, but never skip the conceptual model.

## 5 · Research practices: before you design, know

**Prerequisites:** segmentation and interaction personas, current journey maps (a rough flow is fine), and the ecosystem map of where Nusuk operates.

- **Designer-owned, first:** heuristic evaluation; reports and best practices; Rams "Good Design" evaluation; emotional design; WCAG accessibility; critical incident technique; CSAT and general surveys; design practice assessment.
- **With the CX team:** user interviews; mental models; shadowing, with template and protocol.
- **When needed:** tree testing; usability testing.

## 6 · Intelligent experiences: the AI layer

Adapt, anticipate and assist, while the user stays in control.

| Framework | What it means |
|---|---|
| User-centric adaptive | Data → AI processing → delivery → feedback loop. Tone and suggestions adapt to context and time. |
| Context-aware interaction | Location and time sensing drive the right output: voice, AR or haptics. |
| Predictive personalisation | Anticipate needs before they're asked, with guardrails: transparent, never creepy. |
| Human–AI collaboration | AI as partner, not dictator. The user can always override and ask "why this suggestion?" |
| Ethical and inclusive | Bias mitigation, accessibility, transparency and a sustainability check. |
| Modular experience | Core AI modules and experience templates that plug together. |

References: Google People + AI Guidebook (PAIR), Microsoft HAX, IBM AI Ethics. AI has amendment rights only; humans override, in writing. AI quotes and cites religious content; it never issues a ruling.

## 7 · Quality gates: nothing ships without all eight

1. **Principles:** Intuitive + Engaging + Intelligent.
2. **Intent / desire:** classified, with the motivational realm named.
3. **Behavioural model:** the pattern documented in five parts (Why, What, How, Where, Validate).
4. **Metric alignment:** the KPI pillar, the success threshold, and the link to WMA/WAU.
5. **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA reviewed.
6. **Brand compliance:** DL tokens and components, brand guidelines.
7. **Research grounding:** evidence from research or the knowledge base.
8. **Handoff readiness:** specs, tokens, states, edge cases and responsive behaviour documented.

## Critique and review protocol (14 November 2026)

How a screen, flow or concept is reviewed against the DL. It takes the DX team's heuristic-evaluation method as it stands (account skill `nusuk-dx` › evaluating: severity S0–S4 and ease E0–E4, used verbatim) and adds four checks from the design skills installed with it.

1. **Context before judgement.** Name the job (intent or desire, realm, M2), the person's moment (calm, anxious, sacred window, in a crowd) and the fidelity. Critique only what the fidelity can carry: wireframes get hierarchy, flow, content priority and missing states; high fidelity adds tokens, contrast and brand; a prototype adds transitions and error handling.
2. **Strengths first.** Write down what works before the problems, so a redesign does not lose it.
3. **Two lenses, then the DL.** Nielsen's ten heuristics (does it work?) and Rams' ten principles (should it exist in this form?), then the DL: placement (M9S), template contract (M9T), never-ship (M4), tokens and components (M8, M9), WCAG 2.2 AA (M10), writing (M6), the behavioural pattern and its metric (M3, M5).
4. **One finding, one sentence chain:** what is wrong + the rule it breaks (DL rule, token, component, WCAG criterion, heuristic or Rams principle) + why it matters for this pilgrim + the DL fix, concrete enough to open Figma and make it + severity S0–S4 + ease E0–E4 + whether it was seen or measured, or inferred. No named rule, no finding: that is taste, so it stays out.
5. **Severity honestly.** S4 only when the pilgrim cannot complete the task at all. Anything blocking a rite, a permit, a payment or a departure time is S3 at least. A never-ship item (M4), a dark pattern or invented scarcity, and a WCAG A or AA failure on a core flow are release blockers whatever the count.
6. **Patterns are the finding.** Merge duplicates (one problem in three places is one finding, frequency three). When several findings share a cause, name the cause and route it (M3 diagnostic router); if the conceptual model is wrong, stop rating screens and go back to Map.
7. **Excise and timing.** Count the taps that move the task forward against the taps that don't; the gap is excise. Check feedback timing (M10): nothing silent over 1 s.
8. **Stress-test before agreeing.** Ask the hardest questions, one at a time when the person is there: what happens in Arabic at 200% text, when the network drops at the permit step, for an older first-time pilgrim with a companion, in the 30 seconds before their slot.
9. **Evidence before verdict.** "Passes", "fixed" and "matches Figma" need the screenshot, measurement or file read from the same pass. Without it, say what was not checked.
10. **Close** with "Here's what's strong / Here's what's weak or missing / Here's how I'd sharpen it." Sort by severity, then ease, and list the S3–S4 findings that are E0–E1 on their own: they ship this week.
