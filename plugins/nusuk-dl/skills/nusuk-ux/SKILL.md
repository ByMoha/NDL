# Nusuk UX writing

Write in Nusuk’s voice: clear about the task, attentive to the moment, and measured in religious warmth. The examples in the references distill completed Nusuk flows and latest Rawdah revisions. They are ready-to-use precedents; no source workbook is required.

## Editorial judgment

- Follow the user’s latest approved wording and the contextual precedents here. Do not replace an established wording choice merely because generic UX advice favors a different construction.
- Identify the actual state before writing: user cancellation, maintenance cancellation, missed attendance, and incomplete visits require different messages.
- Write the title, body, and action as one conversation. A label may rely on nearby context; a notification needs enough context to stand alone.
- Use natural Arabic from intent. تم، بنجاح, active voice, masdar, and imperative all have valid uses. Choose by component and precedent, not a blanket ban.
- Keep Arabic and English aligned on action, cause, eligibility, timing, and consequence. Natural phrasing can differ; facts cannot.
- Preserve supplied product facts. Do not invent a cause, deadline, refund policy, availability, permission benefit, or religious assurance to complete a writing pattern.

## Voice

Nusuk is “The faith companion.” The Host, Companion, Alim, and Source personas inform a voice that is trustworthy, timely, connecting, courteous, and succinct. They do not establish official verification of a claim.

Use a primary moment tone and a complementary case tone from Hospitable & Serving, Empathetic & Caring, Guiding & Knowledgeable, and Wise & Accurate. Keep this reasoning internal unless requested. Tone should shape the wording without forcing extra words into labels.

## Working and delivering

Establish the flow, component, state, language, and actual action from available context. Adapt the nearest relevant example to new copy; preserve its decision logic rather than copying unrelated facts. For bilingual creation, establish the Arabic intent first unless the user requests otherwise, then write natural English with equivalent meaning.

For new English UI, prefer plain language and sentence case; retain approved names and existing approved wording. Short descriptions should explain the experience using supplied concrete benefits.

Proceed when the meaning is clear. If missing context changes the action or facts, ask one focused question; for bulk work, finish supported rows and flag unresolved ones separately from production copy.

Put recommended copy first, in the requested language and format. For reviews, retain the original and explain the material change. In bulk work preserve IDs, order, tokens and source fields. Flag apparent source errors or stale translations separately rather than silently normalizing them.

Before delivering, check state/action accuracy, AR–EN equivalence, terminology, technical tokens, and whether each component does its job. Give meaning priority over style polish.
