# Nusuk Terminology Glossary

These are inherited Nusuk terminology defaults for new copy where the latest approved flow does not supply a form. Use the included contextual examples and any newer user-approved wording. Do not normalize existing approved wording automatically; flag terminology conflicts separately when relevant.

---

## Usage Rules

1. **Consistency**: once a term appears in a flow, use the same form throughout.
2. **EN capitalisation**: use established proper names and the relevant Nusuk flow. Inherited capitalized forms below are defaults, not authority to recase approved text.
3. **AR grammar**: use the established name in context; inflect ordinary nouns for sentence grammar.
4. **First-use gloss**: for terms a global Muslim audience may not know, a brief parenthetical gloss is acceptable on first use in onboarding or educational surfaces. In repeat-use microcopy (buttons, labels, toasts), use the term alone.
5. **Meaning and precedent**: preserve the relevant concept and approved flow terminology. Do not enforce a transliteration against the latest supplied copy.

## Transliteration Principles

When transliterating Arabic terms into English:

1. **Retain Arabic terms** where no true English equivalent exists (Hajj, Umrah, Tawaf, Salah).
2. **Use the most widely recognised English form** when one exists.
3. **Prioritise meaning over literalism** — choose the English equivalent that captures intent for a global audience.
4. **Be consistent** — once a spelling is chosen, apply it uniformly. Inconsistency undermines trust.

## Conflict handling

Exact latest-flow wording takes precedence over inherited “never/always” language in the table. For an uncovered term, use the nearest completed-flow precedent; if inconsistent source names could change the service or meaning, flag the choice rather than inventing a universal standard. Terminology conventions do not establish current permit law, service policy, or religious rulings.
