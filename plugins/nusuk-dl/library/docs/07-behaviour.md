# Behavioural patterns and nudges

Every pattern is documented in five parts: **Why** (mechanism), **What** (behaviour), **How** (method), **Where** (in Nusuk), **Validate** (metrics and risks). Apply the relevant pattern before starting any feature, and document it in this format.

## 01 · Attention & cognitive load

- **Why:** Attention and working memory are limited; under stress people simplify, avoid or defer.
- **What:** Decision paralysis, skipped setup, drop-off at too many options.
- **How:** Choice architecture · Chunking and partitioning · Smart defaults as a starting point
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - "Today’s 3 actions", not 30
  - Progressive disclosure: beginner, intermediate, advanced good deeds
  - An editable default daily plan
  - Setup in one question: "What do you want to focus on this week?"
- **Validate:** Time to first action · Completion of the first micro-habit
- **Risk to watch:** Over-simplifying until it feels patronising.

## 02 · Motivation & present bias

- **Why:** People overweight the now; long-term benefit loses to immediate effort.
- **What:** "I’ll read Quran later." The intent is real; follow-through collapses.
- **How:** Present bias awareness · Immediate rewards, with meaning over dopamine · Timely prompts
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - Micro-actions under 30 seconds: one ayah, one thikr set
  - Instant, calm closure that confirms the action counted
  - Context prompts: after prayer, before sleep, on the commute
- **Validate:** Same-day repeat rate · 7-day retention tied to micro-actions
- **Risk to watch:** Turning worship into quick hits instead of meaning.

## 03 · Learning, reinforcement & feedback

- **Why:** Immediate, clear reinforcement strengthens behaviour; uncertainty kills action.
- **What:** Users stop when unsure they’re doing it right, and repeat when success feels clear and safe.
- **How:** Feedback loops · Non-competitive progress · Gentle reinforcement
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - "You’re on track", not "you’re winning"
  - A weekly reflection framed as reassurance
  - Celebrate returning more than streaks
- **Validate:** Re-engagement after a lapse · Notification opt-out rate
- **Risk to watch:** Competitive progress and streak shame.

## 04 · Framing, emotion & meaning

- **Why:** Wording and tone change perception; emotion drives memory and choice more than logic.
- **What:** Fear framing lifts action short-term and damages trust long-term.
- **How:** Framing effects · Gain and loss framing · Peak–end rule
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - Positive, humble framing: guidance, mercy, continuity
  - End every session with calm closure
  - No "don’t miss reward" FOMO
- **Validate:** Trust proxies: app rating, complaints, content reports
- **Risk to watch:** Guilt loops: fast growth, then backlash.

## 05 · Self-control & commitment

- **Why:** Intention is there, but self-control fails under fatigue and distraction.
- **What:** "I want to" but "I didn’t". Bursts of effort, then fall-off.
- **How:** Commitment devices, as a covenant · Consistency · If–then plans
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - "If it’s after Maghrib, then 3 minutes of Quran"
  - "Set a gentle reminder", never "pledge"
  - One-tap restart back into the routine
- **Validate:** Habit continuity: return after lapse · Plan adherence without coercion
- **Risk to watch:** Commitments that feel like an obligation to the app.

## 06 · Social psychology & norms

- **Why:** People look to others for cues, most of all to people "like me".
- **What:** Users follow norms under uncertainty and trust credible messengers.
- **How:** Social norms · Messenger effect · Bandwagon, used carefully
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - "People in your city often…", only if true and privacy-safe
  - Community reassurance: "You’re not alone"
  - No popularity vanity, no trending worship
- **Validate:** Participation in communal journeys
- **Risk to watch:** Comparison, performative religiosity, exclusion.

## 07 · Valuation biases

- **Why:** Value is judged against reference points and ownership, not objectively.
- **What:** The first reference shapes the "right" option; trying something raises its value.
- **How:** Anchoring · Endowment · Decoy: banned in Nusuk
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - Anchor on correctness: trusted sources, clear sequence
  - Experience ownership: a short trial of a guidance feature
  - No decoys, ever
- **Validate:** Conversion to sustained use of guidance modules
- **Risk to watch:** Being perceived as manipulative.

## 08 · Scarcity & urgency

- **Why:** Scarcity raises perceived value; urgency narrows attention and drives impulse.
- **What:** Fast action with less thought, and anxiety.
- **How:** Scarcity principle · Urgency effect
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - Only for operational truth: real capacity and real scheduling limits
  - Never to pressure a spiritual decision
- **Validate:** Drop-offs after urgency prompts
- **Risk to watch:** Reputational damage: people feel pushed.

## 09 · Identity, autonomy & intrinsic motivation

- **Why:** Lasting change comes from identity and intrinsic motivation, not rewards.
- **What:** What lasts becomes "who I am"; control breeds reactance and drop-out.
- **How:** Autonomy, competence, relatedness · Identity-based habits
- **Where in Nusuk:**
  - The user chooses a focus: consistency, knowledge, kindness, family
  - No ranks implying moral superiority
  - "Your intention, your pace"
- **Validate:** Long-term retention without more notification pressure
- **Risk to watch:** Turning "a better Muslim" into a moral score.
## Diagnostic router

The missing piece most teams forget. We're not after a beautiful theory library that doesn't drive decisions: start from the symptom.

| If | Check | Apply |
|---|---|---|
| Adoption is low | Attention and cognitive load | Simplify. Reduce steps. Remove friction. |
| Intention exists, follow-through fails | Present bias and if–then planning | Nudges, commitment devices, implementation intentions. |
| Users stop because they're unsure | Feedback and reassurance | Strengthen feedback loops and reassurance patterns. |
| Backlash or distrust | Framing and autonomy | Re-examine framing. Restore the user's agency. |
| Growth depends on community | Social norms | Social proof, only where it's true and privacy-safe. |

## Nudges and behavioural economics

A nudge changes behaviour predictably without forbidding options or changing incentives, and it is easy to avoid. Libertarian paternalism: help people choose what they judge better, and keep their freedom to choose.

| Nudge | Mechanism | Verdict | In Nusuk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choice architecture | Defaults, partitioning, framing | Use | Editable defaults, three actions not thirty |
| Commitment devices | Present bias, consistency | Use | Soft if–then plans, never a pledge to the app |
| Feedback | Immediate reinforcement | Use | "You're on track", private and non-competitive |
| Framing | Prospect theory | Use | Positive, humble framing; no fear or FOMO |
| Social proof | Social validation | With care | Only when true and privacy-safe; no "trending worship" |
| Anchoring | First reference shapes value | With care | Anchor on correctness: trusted sources, clear sequence |
| Endowment | Ownership raises value | With care | A short trial of a guidance feature |
| Scarcity and urgency | Narrowed attention | Truth only | Real capacity and cutoffs, never for spiritual decisions |
| Decoy effect | Asymmetric dominance | **Banned** | Never make one option look stupid |

## Hook framework

Habits form by cycling through trigger, action, variable reward and investment. In Nusuk, the habit serves the user's own intention, never the app's.

| Stage | Question | In Nusuk |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | What brings them back? | External (a notification) or internal (a routine, a feeling): context prompts after prayer, before sleep, on the commute |
| Action | The simplest step | Micro-actions under 30 seconds, such as one ayah or one thikr set |
| Reward | Fulfilled, gently | Calm closure and the feeling of meaning, not slot-machine surprises |
| Investment | Stored value | A chosen focus and an if–then plan the user owns |

- **Keep:** timely, contextual triggers; one-tap actions and restart flows; personalisation the user controls; celebrating returning.
- **Never in Nusuk:** streaks and streak shame; leaderboards, ranks, points for worship; countdowns, FOMO, loss framing; random rewards that turn worship into "quick hits".

## Evil by design: where Nusuk draws the line

"We are not evil, but we do borrow these principles." Each sin can be applied in a way that harms or in a way that helps.

| Sin | How products use it | Nusuk | Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pride | Badges, public recognition, leaderboards | No | Progress stays private; worship is not a status good |
| Sloth | One-click, autofill, defaults, automation | Yes | Make it easy, with defaults the user can edit |
| Gluttony | Infinite scroll, autoplay, bingeing | No | End sessions with calm closure, not a prompt to do more |
| Greed | Limited-time offers, scarcity | Truth only | Only real capacity and real deadlines |
| Envy | Comparisons, rankings, follower counts | No | No feeds of other people's worship |
| Lust | Aesthetic, aspirational visuals | Yes | Reverent beauty that serves the sacred |
| Wrath | Provocation, controversy | No | Madhab-neutral, always |

- **Dark patterns** (hidden fees, sneaky opt-ins, disguised ads): not used.
- **Persuasive design** (defaults, pre-selection, nudging): used openly and editable.
- **Addictive design** (reward loops, login bonuses): not used; notification opt-out is tracked in the Trust Index.

**The standing test for any mechanic:** would it still be acceptable if the user saw exactly how it works? Does the deadline exist without the campaign? Is there a guardrail metric next to the success metric?
